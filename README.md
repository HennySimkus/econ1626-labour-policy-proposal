# The Cryptographic Agentic Liability Protocol

## Managing the Labour-Market Effects of Autonomous Financial AI

**To:** Deputy Secretary, Workforce and Skills, Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR)

**Policy context:** This proposal advises DEWR on labour-market safeguards that could be incorporated into the implementation of Australia's Digital Assets Framework from April 2027, informed by the 2026 Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence inquiry.

---

# Executive Summary

**Target: ~130–150 words**

> **Purpose:** Give the marker the entire argument in miniature.

The Executive Summary should answer five questions:

### 1. What is happening?

* Autonomous Financial AI is increasingly capable of performing financial tasks independently.

### 2. What is the problem?

* Firms can substitute AI capital for human labour.
* Routine and intermediate financial tasks may be displaced.
* Returns may increasingly flow toward AI-enabled capital.

### 3. Why is government involved?

* Workers face adjustment costs.
* Existing regulation does not specifically identify autonomous financial systems or provide a mechanism for managing their labour-market disruption.

### 4. What is CALP?

* Mandatory digital identity for autonomous financial agents.
* Small levy on qualifying autonomous financial activity.
* Revenue directed toward a Commonwealth AI Transition Fund.

### 5. What does it achieve?

* Accountability
* Better data
* Transition funding
* Preservation of AI productivity gains

**Writing note:** Do not introduce lots of evidence here. The Executive Summary should communicate the argument, not reproduce the analysis.

## 1.2 The Labour-Market Problem

This is where the central argument begins.

Autonomous Financial AI allows firms to substitute **capital for labour**.

### Economic mechanism

**Human performs task → AI performs task → Labour demand for that task falls**

Then introduce the counter-effect:

**AI productivity → cheaper/more productive financial services → demand for complementary skills may increase**

This prevents the argument from becoming:

> "AI = unemployment"

The analysis should instead recognise both **substitution** and **complementarity** effects.

# 2. Labour-Market & Economic Impacts

**Target: ~300 words**

This should be the **main economic analysis section**.

## 2.1 Task Automation → Employment

Ask:

> **What actually gets automated?**

Use evidence to identify vulnerable tasks:

* Transaction processing
* Reconciliation
* Routine compliance
* Reporting
* Monitoring
* Financial analysis

Then explain the economic mechanism:

> AI substitutes for labour in particular tasks rather than necessarily eliminating entire occupations.

Bring in the **Jobs and Skills Australia (JSA)** evidence here:

* **31%** of Australia's workforce is in highly augmentable occupations.
* **4%** is in highly automatable occupations.

This demonstrates understanding of the **task-based approach to automation**.

---

## 2.2 Wages & Labour-Capital Substitution

This is where the payroll/superannuation idea becomes valuable.

### Human labour

A firm pays:

**Wages + superannuation + employment-related costs**

### AI capital

A firm instead pays:

**Software + compute + infrastructure**

### Economic consequence

As firms substitute AI capital for labour, the distribution of income may shift away from **labour compensation** and toward **returns to capital**.

Then discuss potential wage effects:

* Lower demand for routine/intermediate workers
* Higher demand for AI and complementary skills
* Possible wage divergence between workers

**Important:** Do not claim that everyone's wages will fall.

---

## 2.3 Industrial Structure & Distribution

Now zoom out from individual workers.

Autonomous Financial AI could allow firms to:

* Operate with fewer employees
* Scale more cheaply
* Operate continuously
* Reduce marginal costs
* Increase returns to technology and data

### Potential result

Firms with greater access to AI capital may gain an advantage over smaller firms.

### Distributional / Equity Lens

| Potentially Exposed                   | Potential Beneficiaries               |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Routine financial workers             | AI-skilled workers                    |
| Compliance and administrative workers | Technology owners                     |
| Workers needing retraining            | Firms with AI capital                 |
| Workers with less adaptable skills    | Highly specialised AI/finance workers |

This directly addresses the assignment's **distributional and equity requirement**.

# 3. Policy Options

**Target: ~300 words**

You have now **proved the problem**.

The next question is:

> **What should government do about it?**

Use exactly **three options**.

---

## Option 1 — Status Quo

**Mechanism:** Existing regulation applies; no AFAI-specific labour-market mechanism.

**Targets:** All financial firms.

**Cost:** Very low government intervention.

**Advantage:** Maximum flexibility and minimum intervention.

**Problem:** Does not directly address displacement, autonomous-agent identification, or transition funding.

---

## Option 2 — Restrictive Intervention

**Mechanism:**

* Human-in-the-loop requirements
* Limits on autonomous transactions
* Trading caps

**Targets:** High-impact autonomous financial systems.

**Cost:** High regulatory and compliance cost.

**Advantage:** Slows displacement and reduces some risks associated with autonomous systems.

**Problem:** Sacrifices productivity and innovation and could push activity offshore.

---

## Option 3 — Cryptographic Agentic Liability Protocol (CALP)

**Mechanism:**

### 🆔 Digital Identity

Every qualifying autonomous financial agent receives a unique cryptographic identity.

### 📊 Activity Record

Autonomous transactions can be attributed to the responsible entity.

### 💰 Autonomous Activity Levy

A small levy applies to qualifying autonomous financial activity.

### 🎓 Transition Fund

Revenue supports workers experiencing AI-driven labour-market disruption.

**Targets:** Financial firms deploying autonomous systems.

**Cost:** Medium.

**Advantage:** Preserves productivity while making firms contribute toward transition costs.

# 5. Recommended Policy Package

**Target: ~150 words**

This is where you **commit to CALP**.

---

## Component 1 — Digital Agent Identity

Mandatory registration and cryptographic identity for autonomous financial agents operating within the regulated Australian financial system.

**Purpose:**

* Accountability
* Transparency
* Data collection

---

## Component 2 — Autonomous Activity Levy

A small levy on qualifying autonomous financial activity.

**Purpose:**

Capture a portion of the economic gains associated with autonomous activity to help finance labour-market adjustment costs.

### Economic framing

Do **not** describe this as a:

* "Punishment"
* "Automation tax"

Instead, frame it as:

> **AI creates private productivity gains but also creates concentrated transition costs. The levy provides a mechanism for partially socialising those adjustment costs.**

This is a more defensible economic rationale.

---

## Component 3 — Commonwealth AI Transition Fund

Use levy revenue for:

* Retraining
* Job matching
* Transition assistance
* Skills programs
* Support for workers facing displacement

### Labour-market monitoring

**Jobs and Skills Australia (JSA)** could monitor labour-market outcomes and evaluate whether funded programs are producing employment outcomes.

## 6.2 Key Risks & Mitigation

### Risk 1 — Capital Flight

**Risk:** Firms move autonomous activity offshore.

**Mitigation:** Use a proportionate levy and regulated-market access requirements.

---

### Risk 2 — Evasion

**Risk:** Firms classify autonomous systems as ordinary software.

**Mitigation:** Use a functional definition based on **autonomous capability**, rather than how firms brand the technology.

---

### Risk 3 — Over-Regulation

**Risk:** CALP discourages useful AI adoption.

**Mitigation:** Use clear thresholds, proportional requirements and periodic review.

---

### Risk 4 — Poor Transition Outcomes

**Risk:** Funding supports training, but workers do not obtain employment.

**Mitigation:** Allocate funding based on **employment outcomes**, rather than enrolment numbers alone.
